Wardogs is a weird game. It's not Call of Duty. It's not Battlefield, and it's not really Arma either. Yet, somehow, it feels like it takes pieces from all three and makes something that actually works. And the weirdest part? This game is still in Early Access.

So why does WARDOGS work? Why are people playing it? And why do I think this game could actually be more important than people realize?

But, what is Wardogs? Wardogs is a tactical sandbox shooter. At it's core, it is a King of the Hill based game. Which if you have been around the Arma community for a while, you may know that this game is based off of a community made Arma mod.

More then a decade ago, a user by the name Sa-Matra developed a mod for Arma 3, which he called, King of the Hill. The game mode has 3 teams all fighting for a central point on the map. The team with the most players will in the objective will earn points, first to 100 points wins. This is the foundation for Wardogs. Many other games were also started as Arma mods in the beginning. DayZ For example, started out as an Arma 2 mod before the developers of Arma hired the creator of the mod and made it a standalone game, but that's for another video. WARDOGS is taking the the king of the hill format and turning it into an entire standalone game.

But why does this game work? It works because it gives you the freedom to play however you would like, while still giving you a sense of a direction to victory. Almost everything you can do in this game can contribute to the objective in some way. And contributing will reward you with money, creating this sense of selfish selflessness.

You can run around on the frontlines shooting everyone. You can hop in a car and transport people. You can be the doctor your parents always wanted you to be. But no matter what you do, those all contribute to the team. Quite frankly, I think that is very important, because I don't think a good multiplayer game should just give you a weapon and tell you to get kills. It should give you a scenario, give you the tools to deal with it, and then let you figure out how you want to approach it. And I think that is where Wardogs starts to sperate itself from other open world shooters.

Like I mentioned before, there are three teams.. which creates a very interesting dynamic. You could have 2 teams fighting, while the other slips in and takes advantage of both teams being weakened. Or you could have all 3 teams fighting over hot zone. You can even have red and green actually doing things while blue proceeds to crash all of their helicopters.. The game constantly creates these situations where you can't completely predict what's going to happen and that's the beauty of it. The best multiplayer games aren't necessarily the ones with the best mechanics. They're the ones that make you feel like every match is actually unique. That's what WARDOGS can do. Maybe you barely capture the objective. Maybe you get saved by a teammate. Maybe a vehicle completely changes the fight. You're not just playing a match. You're creating a story with every game you play.

Which I thought about doing a video where I document an entire game, if you think that would be cool, let me know down below.

But, just like any other games, Wardogs has a few problems. It's not perfect. There are balancing problems, there are bugs, and there are systems that need some work. There are situations where certain strategies feel much stronger than others. And because the game gives players so much freedom, balancing everything becomes even harder... but what if things aren't actually overpowered? What if there just isn't a counter to it yet?

The foundation of this game is good, and with it only being in early access, there is still a lot more to come for it. Bulkhead knows there are issues, and they actively communicate with the community and provide hot fixes to patch things that are too OP.

I think this game is an overall success, 3 million copies sold in under a month... but this is where I think the game gets really interesting. Because when I say WARDOGS is a success, I don't necessarily mean that it is the best FPS game ever made. I'm talking about the idea of behind the game. The passion the devs had. They took something that works. Something that a community has been playing for over a decade, and improved upon it instead of trying to reinvent multiplayer shooters.

The game isn't for everyone, they don't market it that way either... but this begs the question: Could Wardogs change the industry?

I don't know. Maybe that's a little too ambitious. But I do think there's something interesting happening here. The gaming industry spends a lot of time and money on development. Bulkhead has came out and said that they spent roughly 2.5 million dollars on development [Put on screen that I couldn't find the true source] while AAA studios are spending hundreds of millions on games that just feel like every other shooter. Wardogs is showing that sometimes... the next great idea might already exist. It might just be hiding inside a community. KOTH existed for years. DayZ existed as a mod. Battle Royale existed as a mod.

And now WARDOGS is taking another community-created idea and trying to turn it into something bigger.

And maybe that's the biggest reason I think WARDOGS works. It's not perfect. It's not finished. And I don't think it needs to be. Because the important thing is that the foundation is there. The game gives players freedom. It creates unpredictable situations. It lets people play differently. And most importantly... it gives you a reason to come back and see what happens next. And for a game that's still in Early Access... I think that's pretty damn impressive. That's why WARDOGS works.
